How the multiplication works

A product combines a directly evaluated near part and a hierarchical far part:

y = near interactions * x + far interactions * x

The trial side is the input side, where x lives. The test side is the output side, where y is computed. In Galerkin maps they share a space and tree; Petrov maps may use different ones.

See Construction and execution for the lifecycle of the core and workspace.

Follow the far product

At each source leaf, a moment collection turns input coefficients into expansion coefficients: a moment. I2I transfers combine child moments into parent moments. A small parent expansion can then represent many leaves.

I2O coupling connects well-separated source and receiving nodes. O2O transfers pass received contributions towards the leaves. The test-side collections turn those expansions into y.

Some methods need basis conversion around I2O. Toeplitz coupling, for example, operates in the frequency domain.

The functions in the middle column belong to H2Factory. Numerical data is read from the core; changing moments and scratch belong to the workspace.

Plans, then execution

H2Trees plans decide which nodes and pairs each phase visits. H2Factory schedules those visits and applies the numerical operations.

There are two H2Trees modes: AggregateMode() puts I2O in the downward pass, as above; AggregateTranslateMode() puts it in the upward pass. Aggregation and disaggregation explains their execution. The H2Trees plans guide covers construction.

For a complete implementation, follow Required methods.
